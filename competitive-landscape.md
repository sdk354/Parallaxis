# Competitive Landscape

## Direct Competitors

### WorldMonitor (worldmonitor.app)
- **What it is:** Real-time global situational awareness dashboard — military assets, conflict zones, nuclear sites, live news aggregation, live webcams, AI briefings
- **GitHub stars:** 53k (very popular, well-established)
- **Overlap with Parallaxis:** Interactive conflict map, live news feed, AI insights
- **What it lacks (Parallaxis differentiators):**
  - Rhetoric & propaganda analysis
  - Cross-lingual narrative comparison
  - Perspective layers (Western/Russian/Chinese/Indian/Arabic)
  - State media narrative tracking
  - Narrative mutation tracker
  - Verification & context engine
  - Diplomatic signaling decoder
  - UN voting alignment tracker
  - Debate & logical fallacy detection
- **Key distinction:** WorldMonitor tracks *what is happening*. Parallaxis analyses *how events are being framed, manipulated, and narrativised*.
- **Design note:** WorldMonitor owns the dark military dashboard aesthetic. Parallaxis should deliberately go cleaner and more editorial.

## Indirect Competitors (Editorial Publications)

### thegeopolitics.com
- Online geopolitics magazine, written analysis by academics and policymakers
- No interactive tools, no map, no NLP analysis
- Not a direct competitor — different product type entirely

### geopoliticalmonitor.com
- Editorial publication and political risk consultancy
- Written analysis and risk reports by human experts
- No interactive tools
- Not a direct competitor

### Stratfor (worldview.stratfor.com)
- Premium geopolitical intelligence platform
- Enterprise subscription, very expensive
- Written analysis, not interactive tools
- Not accessible to general public

## Tool-Specific Competitors

### Ground News (ground.news)
- Per-story bias comparison, left/centre/right coverage
- Browser extension available
- Gaps: snapshot not longitudinal, outlet-level not story-level, no geopolitical analysis layer

### AllSides / Media Bias/Fact Check
- Manual bias ratings per outlet
- Human-curated, slow to update
- No interactive tools

### Nobias
- Markets itself as "Fitbit for your media diet"
- Closest to the personal information diet concept
- No geopolitical analytical layer

### prta.eu (Propaganda Research Tool)
- Academic demo from SemEval-2020
- Detects propaganda techniques in text
- Narrow, clunky, not a polished product
- No browser integration, no geopolitical context

### "Check News in 1 Click"
- NLP-powered propaganda detection
- Available in 7 languages
- Narrowly focused on Pro-Kremlin content only

### CooRTweet
- R package for coordinated sharing behaviour detection
- Research tool, not user-facing
- Requires technical expertise

### Hoaxy / OSoMeNet (Indiana University)
- Visualises spread of articles/misinformation on social media
- Tracks spread but not narrative mutation
- Research tool

### DisTrack
- Semi-automatic misinformation tracking in social networks
- Academic tool

### NewsGuard
- Credibility scores for news websites
- Credibility ≠ bias or framing — different purpose

## The Gap Parallaxis Fills
No platform currently exists that combines:
1. Geopolitical conflict tracking with multi-perspective narrative layers
2. Deep NLP analysis tools (propaganda, rhetoric, logical fallacies)
3. Cross-lingual narrative comparison accessible to non-technical users
4. Verification tools with transparent source trails
5. All of the above in one open, accessible platform

The commercial platforms that come closest (Recorded Future, Jane's Intelligence) cost thousands of dollars per year and are designed for enterprise intelligence teams, not the public.
