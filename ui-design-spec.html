Parallaxis

The same event looks different depending on where you stand. The interface has to show that shift — not just label it.

Ground optical survey instrument Not tactical dashboard Not broadsheet revival Rule no verdicts, anywhere
Signature interaction

Two bearings, one fixed event

Each event carries two readings at once: the left half of the disc is how much perspective A covers it, the right half is perspective B. A level disc means the two agree. A lopsided one is a coverage dispute, and an empty half is a blindspot — the most valuable thing the map produces.

2 events unreported under A
severity 2 3 4 5 half-height = that perspective's coverage dashed halo = the two disagree by more than 25 points

An earlier draft encoded divergence by displacing markers. That was wrong: on a map, position already means geography, so a marker nudged 26 pixels east asserts the event is in another country. It also inverted the physics — in real parallax the target holds still while the observer changes station, and the difference between the two bearings is the measurement.

Colour

Hue is reserved, never decorative

Four roles, and nothing else gets a colour. The split that makes it work: cool is the instrument, warm is harm. No piece of interface furniture can ever be mistaken for an escalation signal.

Data encodings do not respond to theme. A severity colour that shifts between light and dark is a broken encoding, so the severity ramp and both channel hues are fixed values in every theme — only the document surface adapts.

The consequential one

Perspectives are never colour-coded

Give Russian media red and Western media blue, and the stylesheet has just made a political claim the methodology chapter explicitly refuses to make. This is the decision most likely to be challenged in a viva, and the easiest one to get wrong by accident, because every mapping tool in the world hands out a categorical palette by default.

So no perspective holds a persistent colour anywhere in the platform. In divergence mode the two under comparison are assigned to channel A and channel B, with a swap control sitting visibly in the interface. Making the arbitrariness operable is more honest than merely intending it — the user can prove to themselves that the colours mean nothing.

A second reason: fixed hues imply the perspective set is closed and canonical, which contradicts the open decision in scope-decisions.md to rethink the layer set entirely.

Uncertainty

Confidence is a stroke, not a colour

A red highlight is a verdict, whatever the tooltip says. So findings never colour the text — they underline it, and the line breaks up as confidence falls. Commentary sits in the margin, so the source is read on its own terms first.

Voice

The interface is where hedging leaks

A carefully calibrated model output, presented under an alarming label, has been un-hedged by the interface. Findings are observations with stated limits — never verdicts, never alerts.

Rejected

Directions ruled out on purpose

Dark tactical dashboard
Owned by WorldMonitor, and it dresses the platform as the institutions it exists to offer an alternative to.
Warm editorial revival
Cream ground, high-contrast serif, terracotta accent. Adjacent to what the brief asked for, which is exactly why it needs excluding out loud rather than avoiding by instinct.
Traffic-light severity
Fails colour vision deficiency, fails greyscale print, and imports moral valence the composite score does not claim.
Inline highlight annotation
Visually hostile at density, epistemically overconfident, and already the approach of the weaker academic tools in the space.
Fixed per-perspective colour
An editorial claim made through CSS. The most consequential rejection here.
